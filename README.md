# Clubfoot-analysis
Clubfoot Data Analysis - Business Analyst Internship Project | Power BI

## Project Overview
This project was completed as part of my business Analyst Internship.
The project analyzes reported clubfoot cases and treatment - centre distribution across six Nigerian states using power BI.

## Business Questions
- Which states have the highest reported number of cases?
- Which states have the highest reported prevalence/rate?
- Which states have the largest estimated number of expected cases based on births?
- How do rankings change when population/births are considered?
- How does estimated need compare with available treatment service?
- Where are treatment centres geographically distributed?

  ## Key Findings
  - 495 total reported cases
  - 426 cases reported in Akwa Ibom
  - 69 cases reported cases from cross river
  - 86.06% of reported cases were from Akwa Ibom
  - Reported cases were recorded in 2 of 6 states
  - 9 Treatment centres identified
 
   ## Data Limitations
  The dataset used for this internship project is private. Prevalence and expected annual cases could not be calculated because the
   required denominator and incidence data were not available.

  ## KEY INSIGHTS
  - Cases were highly concentrated geographically.
    495 reported cases were recorded across the six states in the dataset.
    Akwa Ibom accounted for 426 cases (86.06%), while cross river accounted for the remaining 69 cases.
    Four states had no reported cases in the dataset.

  - Reported cases should not be interpreted as prevalence.
    The dataset records reported/hospital cases,but does not provide the population denominators required to calculate true prevalence.
    Therefore, differences between states may reflect differences in population,reporting, access to healthcare,referral patterns, or
     case detection, rather than actual differences in disease burden.

  - Treatment access may not match potential need.
    The distribution of treatment centres should be compared with the number of expected cases and reported cases in each state.
    State with higher potential need but fewer treatment facilities may represent service-access gaps.

  - Patient movement can indicate access challenges.
    Future data collection should include consistent population, annual births, treatment enrolment, treatment outcomes, referral
     sources, patient location, and treatment centre capacity.
    This would allow more accurate measurement of prevalence, estimated need, treatment coverage, and unmet need.
 
 ## RECOMMENDATIONS
 - Prioritize areas with higher reported/estimated need.
   Use the available case and birth-related information to identify states that may require greater attention and further assessment.
 - Investigate states with zero reported cases.
   Four of the six states recorded zero reported cases. this should not automatically be interpreted as zero clubfoot cases. Further investigation
   could determine whether this reflects low case detection, limited reporting, or genuinely low occurrence.
 - Review treatment centre distribution.
   Compare the location of available treatment centres with reported cases and estimated need to identify areas where additional treatment services maybe
   required.
 - Strengthen data collection.
   future data collection should consistently capture variables such as state population, annual births, treatment outcomes, and treatment
   centre capacity. This would allow more accurate calculations of prevalence, treatment coverage, and unmet need.
